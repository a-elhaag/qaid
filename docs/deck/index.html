What the accountant does with it

  • Every client sends receipts as photos and chat messages.
  • The accountant chases the missing ones, then types each one in.
  • Manual receipt entry: 3–5 minutes each, 1–3% errors.

Who we sell to

Small businesses keep their accountant. So we serve the accountant.

Why they will not switch to software alone

  • The state is adding compliance, not removing it. The simplified tax regime (Law 6 of 2025, turnover up to EGP 20m) is conditional on e-invoice and e-receipt compliance.
  • The e-invoice threshold dropped from EGP 500,000 to 250,000 a year, so more small businesses must register.
  • E-invoices and e-receipts cover what a business issues. The paper from its suppliers is still a photo for the accountant to read.

EY; Andersen; ClearTax Egypt. sourced The last point is our reasoning. reasoning

How the work is balanced

Two readers, one referee, a human on top

Who does what

AI reads text, proposes a category, drafts a reminder.

Code adds VAT, totals, duplicates, price jumps, client status. Every figure comes from stored records.

Human confirms. Only confirmed entries count.

Nothing is fixed silently. If the readers disagree, the entry is flagged.

Two lanes

A: Cohere Parse v5 reads layout, GPT-6 structures it.
B: GPT-6 vision reads the image.

fuse() compares field by field: agree, resolved, disputed, or single if one lane is down.

The Parse deployment hit its rate limit (HTTP 429) during testing. The pipeline fell back to one lane and said so.

Fast architecture

The client never waits. The lanes run side by side.

One receipt, seconds (measured)

Lane times from single runs (vision 2.9–4.3 s, Parse 5.9 s). One-after-the-other is the sum, our arithmetic. Fused median 6.5 s, max 8.0 s over 10 receipts.

  • Upload returns at once. Reading continues after the response (after()).
  • Postgres job queue with skip locked, retries and a dead state. A one-minute sweep is the backstop.
  • Parallel lanes. Time is the slower lane, not the sum.
  • Live board. It refreshes itself every 4 s, so the accountant sees the row appear.
  • Phone checks first. Blur, light and crop are caught before upload, so bad photos never reach the models.

what we are not claiming

  • Receipts were clean renders, not phone photos, and only 10 of them. Real accuracy will be lower.
  • One date came out as 2021 instead of 2026 and one Arabic name lost a letter. Both were chosen between two disagreeing readers, so the next fix is to send every such pick to the accountant.
  • The Parse reader was rate limited, so 5 of 50 fields were single-lane. Production needs more quota.

Business impact

More clients per bookkeeper, same team

If Qaid takes a client from 5 h to 4 h a month, a bookkeeper moves from 25 to about 31 clients: +6.

Egyptian bookkeeper pay is about EGP 6,000 a month (Glassdoor median), so an hour costs about EGP 37.5. The 5 h and 4 h figures are US workload numbers.

Pricing idea: EGP 25–35 per client per month, or a flat office plan, until a real fee is known.

Honest limits and next

What Qaid does not do, and what we do next

  • Prepares only. It does not file taxes, talk to banks or send WhatsApp messages.
  • Printed receipts, EGP only. Handwriting is out of scope.
  • VAT and insurance rates in config are still marked “to verify”.
  • Test with one real accounting office and real phone photos.
  • Flag every disputed or resolved field for review.
  • Raise the Parse quota, then pilot with a handful of clients.
